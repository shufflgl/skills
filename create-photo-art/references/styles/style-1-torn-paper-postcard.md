# Style 1: Torn-paper screen-print postcard

## Inputs

- Identify the principal subject conservatively from visible evidence and use a short, specific subject name for the label.
- Accept an optional place and date. Do not ask for them merely to fill the footer. Show the footer only when the user supplied both values.

## Composition

1. Use an exact 3:4 portrait canvas.
2. Preserve the complete original photograph in the upper region, approximately 55% of the canvas height. Retain the subject's defining features, scene atmosphere, natural light, and source color. Prefer contain, natural background extension, or minimal edge cropping to stretching, distortion, retouching, or invented subject detail.
3. End the photo with a natural irregular hand-torn edge with clearly visible paper fibers.
4. Leave an ivory paper gap below the tear so the photograph and illustration remain fully separated. Set the identified subject name there in small vintage typewriter lettering.
5. In the lower region, draw a small borderless illustration derived from the subject. Preserve its recognizable silhouette while simplifying secondary detail. Use screen-print texture, slight grain, subtle ink irregularity, and a restrained lo-fi print finish.
6. Extract two to four muted, low-saturation colors from the photograph. Do not introduce an unrelated accent palette.
7. Limit the illustration to roughly half of the lower region's height and surround it with generous empty space.
8. Place one understated distressed blue-black circular postal cancellation on the right side of the torn boundary, bridging the photo and paper regions.
9. When both place and date are supplied, add one bottom caption in the generic pattern `LOCATION · PLACE DATE · DATE`, replacing `PLACE` and `DATE` with the supplied values exactly.
10. Give the full piece a warm old-paper character with generous negative space and a restrained, premium editorial feel.

## Exclusions

Do not add a postage stamp, coordinates, outer border, three-dimensional mockup, heavy drop shadow, curled corner, extra seal or cancellation, repeated text, or any watermark. Do not let the lower illustration become a second full-bleed scene.

## Verify

Confirm the exact 3:4 portrait ratio, approximately 55/45 photo-to-lower-region balance, faithful upper photograph, fibrous torn edge, clean ivory separation, correct subject label, recognizable small illustration, two-to-four-color palette, one boundary cancellation, and absence of every excluded element. Confirm the place/date footer is either complete or entirely absent.

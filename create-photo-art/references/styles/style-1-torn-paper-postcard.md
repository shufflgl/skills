# Style 1: Torn-paper screen-print postcard

## Inputs

- Identify the principal subject conservatively from visible evidence and use a short, specific subject name for the label.
- Accept an optional place and date. Do not ask for them merely to fill the footer. Show the footer only when the user supplied both values.

## Creation modes

- **Photo-integrated:** use the photographic and illustrated regions described below.
- **Art-only:** omit the original photo completely. Build the entire canvas from warm ivory paper, a restrained torn-paper layer, the subject label, one small screen-print interpretation, and the single cancellation. Derive the subject and palette from a supplied reference or text brief. Keep generous negative space and do not leave a blank placeholder where the photo would have been.

## Composition

1. Use the selected aspect ratio. Follow the shared ratio guidance when choosing the division axis.
2. In photo-integrated mode, preserve the complete original photograph in the leading region, approximately 55% of the canvas along the division axis. Retain the subject's defining features, scene atmosphere, natural light, and source color. Prefer contain, natural background extension, or minimal edge cropping to stretching, distortion, retouching, or invented subject detail.
3. In photo-integrated mode, end the photo with a natural irregular hand-torn edge with clearly visible paper fibers. In art-only mode, apply that edge to the internal paper layer instead.
4. In photo-integrated mode, leave an ivory paper gap beyond the tear so the photograph and illustration remain fully separated. Set the identified subject name there in small vintage typewriter lettering. In art-only mode, use the torn edge as a quiet paper-layer transition within the full artistic composition.
5. Draw a small borderless illustration derived from the subject. Preserve its recognizable silhouette while simplifying secondary detail. Use screen-print texture, slight grain, subtle ink irregularity, and a restrained lo-fi print finish.
6. Extract two to four muted, low-saturation colors from the reference photo or the brief's requested atmosphere. Do not introduce an unrelated accent palette.
7. In photo-integrated mode, limit the illustration to roughly half of the artistic region's depth. In art-only mode, keep it deliberately small relative to the full canvas. Surround it with generous empty space in either mode.
8. Place one understated distressed blue-black circular postal cancellation at the torn boundary. Keep it subordinate and avoid implying a second stamp or seal.
9. When both place and date are supplied, add one bottom caption in the generic pattern `LOCATION · PLACE DATE · DATE`, replacing `PLACE` and `DATE` with the supplied values exactly.
10. Give the full piece a warm old-paper character with generous negative space and a restrained, premium editorial feel.

## Exclusions

Do not add a postage stamp, coordinates, outer border, three-dimensional mockup, heavy drop shadow, curled corner, extra seal or cancellation, repeated text, or any watermark. Do not let the lower illustration become a second full-bleed scene.

## Verify

Confirm the selected ratio, fibrous torn edge, clean ivory paper, correct subject label, recognizable small illustration, two-to-four-color palette, one cancellation, and absence of every excluded element. In photo-integrated mode, also confirm the approximately 55/45 balance and faithful source photo. In art-only mode, confirm no original-photo region or empty photo placeholder remains. Confirm the place/date footer is either complete or entirely absent.

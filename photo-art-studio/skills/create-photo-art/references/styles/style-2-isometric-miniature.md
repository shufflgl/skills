# Style 2: Isometric miniature editorial poster

## Output set

Create one independent poster for every supplied photo. Never combine multiple photos into one composition.

## Creation modes

- **Photo-integrated:** pair a faithful photographic region with an equal isometric miniature region, using the division axis defined by the selected ratio.
- **Art-only:** omit the original photograph and use the full canvas as a quiet editorial field for one isometric miniature scene. Derive the subject, narrative relationship, and palette from the reference photo or text brief. Rebalance the title and micro-annotations around the miniature without preserving an empty half.

## Composition

1. Use the selected aspect ratio. In photo-integrated mode, split the canvas into two strictly equal regions along the shared ratio guidance's division axis.
2. In the photographic region, retain the original photograph's subject structure, real texture, natural light, and original color atmosphere. Apply only subtle premium photographic grading suitable for an art magazine, independent publication, or exhibition photograph. Extend sky, ground, or environmental background naturally when needed, but never stretch, distort, or alter the subject. Skip this region entirely in art-only mode.
3. Extract the most recognizable subject, silhouette, posture, and narrative relationship. Rebuild them in the artistic region, or across the full canvas in art-only mode, as a refined minimal isometric illustration resembling a miniature three-dimensional model or architectural maquette displayed on paper.
4. Do not mechanically reproduce every photographic detail. Use simplified masses, clean edges, clear hierarchy, and miniature scene relationships while keeping the subject immediately recognizable.
5. Place the miniature on a small paper plane or restrained base amid generous negative space. Establish scale and solitude through mass contrast, edge cropping, a light shadow, and very few tiny details. Optional supporting elements must not become a second focal point.
6. Derive the palette from the reference photo or the brief's requested atmosphere. Preserve its defining colors and warm-cool relationship, then reduce, soften, and unify them into a limited palette. Use tonal steps, neighboring hues, and small color differences to separate forms; add no unrelated fixed color scheme.
7. Use fine ink lines, flat color fields, slight shadows, and subtle paper grain for structure and depth. Avoid glossy or plastic 3D rendering.
8. Integrate a short English title derived conservatively from the photo's subject, place, action, state, or mood. Add no more than a very small amount of numbering, short copy, or micro-annotation. Keep typography thin, restrained, and modern; align it with an empty edge, the base, a horizontal axis, or the isometric structure.

## Exclusions

Avoid cartoon styling, high-saturation color clashes, colors unrelated to the source, plastic 3D effects, crowded scenes, a second focal point, e-commerce styling, and obvious template aesthetics.

## Verify

Confirm the selected ratio, recognizable isometric interpretation, coherent limited palette, generous negative space, restrained paper-model depth, concise English typography, one output per input, and absence of every excluded tendency. In photo-integrated mode, confirm the 50/50 split and faithful undistorted photo. In art-only mode, confirm the full canvas is intentionally composed and contains no original-photo region.
